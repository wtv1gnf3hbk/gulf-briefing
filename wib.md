**Yemen leadership succession.** Former president Abdrabbuh Mansour Hadi died at 80, [AP News reported](https://apnews.com/article/yemen-expresident-abdrabbuh-mansour-hadi-died-b3afd7b3679af14d335578e9560c7cd8). The internationally recognized leader led Yemen's transition following the Arab Spring but was ousted by Houthis in 2015. His death removes a symbolic figurehead for the Saudi-backed government as the country remains divided between IRG forces, Houthis, and the Southern Transitional Council.

**Iran-US ceasefire deal emerges.** The US and Iran reached a tentative agreement to extend their ceasefire and begin nuclear talks, [according to AP](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-war-oil-may-28-2026-8f5ed2813ba63df7ae9ccbe991688d29) and [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/iran-us-trade-air-strikes-after-trump-dismisses-report-hormuz-deal-2026-05-28/). Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan praised the US decision to "give diplomacy a chance" to restore security in the Strait of Hormuz following escalating military exchanges since February.

**Kuwait faces hostile strikes.** Kuwaiti forces intercepted drone and missile attacks, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/kuwaiti-army-says-air-defences-intercepting-hostile-missile-drone-attacks-2026-05-28/). The attacks drew widespread Arab condemnation, with Iraq, the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, and Palestine all expressing solidarity with Kuwait and calling for regional restraint.

**Hajj season concludes safely.** Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman held the annual reception for pilgrims at Mina Palace following completion of Hajj rituals. Saudi officials reported over 1.27 million health services provided to pilgrims, with advanced monitoring systems including smart bracelets tracking vital signs to ensure pilgrim safety during the holy season.

**Oman navigates diplomatic pressure.** US Treasury Secretary warned Muscat about potential Strait of Hormuz tolls following Trump's threats against the sultanate, [according to CNN Arabic](https://arabic.cnn.com/middle-east/article/2026/05/28/oman-has-assured-us-it-wont-toll-strait-of-hormuz-bessent-says). Oman's Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi met with Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister, emphasizing diplomatic engagement while maintaining the country's traditional mediation role.

**UAE advances AI leadership.** The UAE ranked first globally in artificial intelligence talent concentration growth at 121% between 2019 and 2025, Sheikh Abdullah bin Zayed announced. The government launched new AI assistants for procurement, taxes, and customer services as part of a broader digital transformation affecting half of government operations.

**Qatar denies Iran payment reports.** Foreign Ministry spokesman Majed Al Ansari rejected reports that Qatar offered $12 billion to Iran to secure a deal, calling them "simply not true" and designed to "sabotage diplomatic efforts toward regional de-escalation and stability."

**Tankers transit Hormuz secretly.** Three oil and LNG tankers exited the Strait of Hormuz with transponders turned off, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/three-oil-lng-tankers-exit-hormuz-with-transponders-off-2026-05-28/). The unusual practice reflects heightened maritime security concerns following recent military escalations between Iran and the US in the strategic shipping lane.

**Air cargo demand rises despite tensions.** Global air cargo demand increased 4% in April despite Middle East disruptions, the International Air Transport Association reported. The resilience demonstrates the sector's ability to adapt to regional conflicts affecting major shipping routes.

**Bahrain maintains regional security.** Interior Ministry rescued five children from a house fire in Aali without casualties. The kingdom continued condemning Iranian attacks on Kuwait and reaffirmed its commitment to Gulf security cooperation through various diplomatic channels.
