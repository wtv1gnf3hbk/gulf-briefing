**Gulf Brief**
*April 13, 2026*

1. **US naval blockade.** The United States threatened to blockade all Iranian ports starting Monday, including the Strait of Hormuz, following the collapse of peace talks in Islamabad. Oil prices [surged past](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/4/13/oil-prices-surge-past-103-a-barrel-after-us-announces-blockade-of-iran?traffic_source=rss) $103 per barrel. The move escalated tensions after marathon negotiations between Washington and Tehran failed to reach agreement.

2. **Saudi pipeline restored.** Saudi Arabia restored full capacity on its East-West oil pipeline to 7 million barrels per day after recent attacks, according to [Reuters reports](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/saudi-arabia-restores-full-capacity-east-west-oil-pipeline-7-million-bpd-after-2026-04-12/). Princess Reema confirmed that "energy facilities and East–West pipeline affected by the attacks have recovered and restored operational capacity."

3. **Saudi-Iraq diplomatic tensions.** Saudi Arabia summoned Iraq's ambassador over continued drone threats from Iraqi territory, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/saudi-arabia-summons-iraq-envoy-over-drone-threats-iraqi-territory-2026-04-12/). The Saudi Foreign Ministry's official Twitter account confirmed it summoned Ambassador Safiya Talib Al-Suhail "against the backdrop of the continued attacks and blatant threats that targeted the Kingdom and the sisterly Gulf states."

4. **Oman energy expansion.** Oman launched a bidding round for five new oil and gas blocks as the sultanate seeks to expand hydrocarbon production, [according to Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/oman-launches-bidding-round-five-oil-gas-blocks-2026-04-12/). The announcement came as global energy markets remained volatile following regional tensions.

5. **Saudi-Yemen talks.** Saudi envoy Mohammed AlJabir announced he would visit Sanaa with an Omani delegation "with the aim of consolidating the truce and ceasefire, supporting the prisoner exchange process." AlJabir emphasized on Twitter that nearly 2 million Yemenis work in Saudi Arabia, transferring over $4 billion annually to support 15 million family members in Yemen.

6. **Gulf stocks decline.** Gulf equity markets slid as US-Iran talks faltered and ceasefire concerns resurfaced, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/gulf-stocks-slide-us-iran-talks-falter-ceasefire-doubts-resurface-2026-04-12/). The uncertainty reflected broader regional concerns about military escalation following the breakdown in diplomatic negotiations.

7. **UK-Oman diplomatic contact.** Sultan Haitham received a telephone call from Britain's Prime Minister, according to [Times of Oman](https://timesofoman.com//article/170566-hm-the-sultan-receives-telephone-call-from-prime-minister-of-uk). Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi urged extending the ceasefire, tweeting that "success may require" continued negotiations despite the current tensions.

8. **Iran-Russia coordination.** Iranian President Pezeshkian discussed regional stability with Russian President Putin, the [Times of Oman reported](https://timesofoman.com//article/170565-pezeshkian-putin-discuss-regional-stability). The talks occurred as Tehran faced increasing international pressure over its nuclear program and regional activities.

9. **Oman wheat harvest.** Wheat harvesting began in Al Najd province of Dhofar Governorate, highlighting Oman's agricultural development efforts [Times of Oman reported](https://timesofoman.com//article/170562-wheat-harvest-ongoing-in-al-najd-province-of-dhofar-governorate). The harvest represents part of the sultanate's food security initiatives.

10. **Saudi defense meetings.** Deputy Defense Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman met with defense ministers from Greece and Britain, discussing regional developments and "Iran's continued flagrant attacks on the Kingdom and other regional states." He condemned Iranian actions in separate meetings with both Nikos Dendias and John Healey.
