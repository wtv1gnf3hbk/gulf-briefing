1. **US-Iran nuclear agreement.** The United States and Iran reached a tentative deal to extend the ceasefire and begin new nuclear talks, according to AP and Reuters reports. US Vice President Vance said the parties are "very close" to an agreement but "not there yet," while Trump indicated the US was "not satisfied" with the current [deal yet](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/us-iran-reach-deal-need-trumps-final-approval-axios-reports-2026-05-28/).

2. **Former Yemen president dies.** Former Yemeni President Abdrabbuh Mansour Hadi died at age 80, AP News reported. The IRG government mourned the "great fighter Field Marshal," with Information Minister Al-Eryani calling his death the end of a "national march full of giving, struggle, and notable patriotic stances." Hadi led Yemen's internationally-recognized [government during](https://apnews.com/article/yemen-expresident-abdrabbuh-mansour-hadi-died-b3afd7b3679af14d335578e9560c7cd8) the civil war.

3. **Saudi oil price cuts expected.** Saudi Arabia may lower July oil prices to Asia as demand weakens, according to Reuters. The kingdom has been managing production cuts as part of OPEC+ efforts to support global oil markets. Prince Faisal bin Farhan praised Trump's decision to give "diplomacy a chance" regarding regional [negotiations](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/saudi-arabia-may-lower-july-oil-prices-asia-demand-weakens-2026-05-29/).

4. **Oman-US tensions over Strait of Hormuz.** The United States warned Oman against facilitating tolls for the Strait of Hormuz, Reuters reported. The warning comes as regional maritime tensions continue following months of disruptions to shipping routes. Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi recently held discussions with Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister, reaffirming "the importance of diplomatic engagement on [all fronts](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/us-warns-oman-not-engage-facilitating-tolls-strait-hormuz-2026-05-28/)."

5. **Hajj season concludes successfully.** Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman received congratulations from Gulf leaders on the successful completion of the 1447 Hajj season. KUNA reported that Kuwait's Amir, Crown Prince, and Prime Minister all sent congratulatory messages. The Saudi Interior Ministry highlighted health leaders' participation and meetings with [foreign delegations](https://kuna.net.kw/ArticleDetails.aspx?id=3305696).

6. **UAE AI talent growth.** The UAE ranked first globally in artificial intelligence talent concentration, with 121% growth between 2019 and 2025, according to Sheikh Abdullah bin Zayed. He described the figure as embodying "the vision of a leadership that has made building people, attracting minds, and creating the future a solid [national approach](https://twitter.com/ABZayed)."

7. **Iran denies US aircraft downing.** The US military denied Iranian state TV claims that American aircraft were destroyed near Iran's Bushehr province. Iran's naval forces said they were conducting reconnaissance work across the Red Sea when an American warship "opened fire in a hysterical manner," according to Houthi spokesman Mohammed [Abdulsalam](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/irans-state-tv-says-us-aircraft-destroyed-irans-bushehr-no-confirmation-us-2026-05-28/).

8. **Bahrain condemns Iranian attack on Kuwait.** The Bahrain Foreign Ministry strongly condemned what it described as an Iranian attack on Kuwait. The statement emphasized Bahrain's solidarity with "the sisterly State of Kuwait" following the incident. Regional tensions have escalated as various Gulf states have [expressed concern](https://twitter.com/bahdiplomatic) about Iranian activities.

9. **Yemen peace efforts continue.** Saudi Ambassador to Yemen Mohammed Al-Jabir announced plans to visit Sanaa with an Omani delegation to consolidate the truce and ceasefire. The visit aims to support prisoner exchanges and discuss dialogue between Yemeni components to reach "a comprehensive and sustainable political [solution](https://twitter.com/mohdsalj) in Yemen."

10. **Qatar denies Iran payment reports.** Qatar's Foreign Ministry spokesperson Majed Al-Ansari rejected reports suggesting Qatar "offered" $12 billion to Iran to secure a deal. He called the reports "simply not true" and said they were being circulated by "parties attempting to sabotage the deal and undermine ongoing diplomatic efforts toward regional [de-escalation](https://twitter.com/majedalansari)."
