**Gulf Brief**

1. **Iran-Hormuz standoff.** Iranian officials declared the country will never cede control of the Strait of Hormuz, as a US naval blockade near Iranian ports continues, according to the BBC. Kuwait declared force majeure on oil shipments following the strait's closure, while Bloomberg reported traffic [slowed to](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-20/hormuz-tracker-traffic-slows-to-trickle-as-opening-hopes-dashed) a trickle after Iran ended a brief reopening attempt.

2. **UAE terror plot.** Multiple Arab states condemned what they described as an Iranian-linked terrorist plot against UAE security and stability. Oman officially expressed solidarity with the UAE following the thwarted scheme, per Times of Oman, while Egypt, Bahrain, and other regional allies [issued condemnations](https://timesofoman.com//article/170871-oman-condemns-threats-to-uae-security-reaffirms-solidarity) through their foreign ministries.

3. **Xi-MBS call.** Chinese President Xi Jinping urged Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman to keep the Strait of Hormuz open during a phone call, Reuters reported. The conversation addressed regional tensions as Beijing seeks to protect its energy supplies, with Xi calling for a ceasefire in broader regional [conflicts affecting](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-20/china-s-xi-urges-ceasefire-full-hormuz-transit-in-saudi-call) global trade routes.

4. **US-UAE support.** White House economic advisor Kevin Hassett said the US would likely provide financial assistance to the UAE if needed during the current regional crisis, Bloomberg reported. The statement came as Middle Eastern stock markets fell due to [renewed tensions](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/uae-bourses-ease-renewed-us-iran-tensions-2026-04-20/) between Washington and Tehran affecting Gulf financial centers.

5. **Saudi weapons diplomacy.** Pakistan placed a $1.5 billion weapons sale to Sudan on hold after Saudi Arabia objected to the transaction, according to Reuters sources. The move reflects Riyadh's concerns about arms flows to conflict zones, as the kingdom seeks to [position itself](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/pakistan-places-15-billion-sudan-weapons-sale-hold-after-saudi-objection-sources-2026-04-20/) as a regional peace broker.

6. **Palestine conference.** Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan co-chaired an international conference in Paris on Palestinian statehood with his French counterpart. The kingdom reaffirmed its "firm and unwavering" position on establishing a Palestinian state, with Prince Faisal praising France's recognition [plans for](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/21/cuba-confirms-meeting-with-us-official-wants-end-to-trumps-energy-blockade) Palestine in his official Twitter feed.

7. **Iraq-Syria border.** A border crossing between Iraq and Syria reopened for the first time in more than a decade, AP News reported. The development signals improving regional connectivity as both countries work to normalize relations following years of conflict and [political instability](https://apnews.com/article/iraq-syria-border-crossing-4fa0f36b5cd3ffe82bb17d992f1c07bb) affecting cross-border trade.

8. **Yemen Houthi-IRG talks.** The internationally recognized Yemeni government engaged in prisoner exchange negotiations, according to Houthi negotiator Mohammed Abdulsalam's Twitter posts. The talks included agreements on banking procedures and airline operations, suggesting limited [diplomatic progress](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/4/21/global-sumud-flotilla-disrupts-cargo-ship-bound-for-israel) despite ongoing military tensions.

9. **Global oil reserves.** Strategic petroleum reserves worldwide totaled 2.5 billion barrels at the end of 2025, the US Energy Information Administration reported via Reuters. The data comes as Gulf producers face supply chain disruptions from the Hormuz crisis, potentially affecting [global energy](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/global-strategic-oil-inventory-totaled-25-billion-barrels-end-2025-eia-says-2026-04-20/) security calculations.

10. **Qatar-Oman coordination.** Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani met with Oman's Sultan Haitham bin Tariq to discuss regional security challenges. The leaders emphasized diplomatic solutions and coordination between their countries, according to the Qatari PM's Twitter account, as both nations maintain [neutral positions](https://timesofoman.com//article/170870-alcaraz-sabalenka-win-laureus-awards-1) in regional conflicts.
