**Gulf Daily Brief**

1. **Saudi oil facilities attacked.** Iranian strikes cut Saudi Arabia's oil production capacity by 600,000 barrels per day and halted East-West Pipeline flows, according to [Reuters reports](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/operational-activities-halted-several-energy-facilities-saudi-arabia-state-news-2026-04-09/). Prince Khalid bin Salman condemned Iran's "continued flagrant attacks on the Kingdom" in meetings with Greek and British defense ministers. Kuwait strongly denounced the targeting of Saudi energy infrastructure.

2. **Strait of Hormuz remains blocked.** Shipping through the vital waterway stayed at a trickle despite the US-Iran ceasefire, with ADNOC CEO saying the strait "is shut, must reopen without conditions," [Reuters reports](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/strait-hormuz-is-shut-must-reopen-without-conditions-uae-oil-giant-adnocs-ceo-2026-04-09/). Oil prices extended gains following the biggest drop since 2020. Trump criticized Iran's handling of the strait as "not the agreement we have."

3. **Starmer visits Gulf for ceasefire talks.** UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer traveled to the Gulf to shore up the "fragile" US-Iran ceasefire, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/4/10/uk-pm-keir-starmer-visits-gulf-to-shore-up-fragile-us-iran-ceasefire?traffic_source=rss). Starmer said the Iran conflict shows Britain "must take a new path." Israeli attacks on Lebanon threatened to undermine the truce negotiations. Gulf states welcomed the ceasefire warily as doubts and attacks persisted.

4. **UAE weighs Iran asset freeze.** The UAE considered freezing Iranian assets, which would be a "double-edged sword" given Dubai's role as a financial hub for Iranian trade, [Reuters analysis](https://www.reuters.com/commentary/breakingviews/uae-iran-asset-freeze-would-be-double-edged-sword-2026-04-09/) suggested. China limited support for Iran due to Xi Jinping's $270 billion Middle East investments. The UAE Defense Ministry confirmed no air threats detected from Iran on April 9.

5. **Iran rejects enrichment limits.** Iranian nuclear chief ruled out uranium enrichment restrictions, [Times of Oman reports](https://timesofoman.com//article/170473-iranian-nuclear-chief-rules-out-uranium-enrichment-restrictions). The stance complicates efforts to revive nuclear negotiations alongside the ceasefire. Iran's position hardens as regional tensions remain high despite the temporary truce with Washington.

Kuwait Airport remained closed despite earlier reports of flight resumptions. The small Gulf state positioned itself defensively as regional missile and drone threats persisted following the fragile ceasefire.

7. **Saudi Foreign Minister holds talks.** Prince Faisal bin Farhan spoke by phone with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Spanish Foreign Minister José Manuel Albarez, and Pakistani Deputy Prime Minister Muhammad Ishaq Dar, according to official Saudi MFA posts. The conversations focused on regional developments and Iran's continued attacks. Saudi Arabia maintained diplomatic pressure for broader international condemnation of Iranian actions.

8. **Yemen IRG officials visit Saudi envoy.** Saudi Ambassador to Yemen Mohammed Al-Jaber announced plans to visit Sanaa with an Omani delegation to consolidate the truce and discuss prisoner exchanges, according to his [social media post](https://twitter.com/mohdsalj). Al-Jaber highlighted that nearly 2 million Yemenis work in Saudi Arabia, transferring over $4 billion annually to families back home. The visit aims to support comprehensive political solutions between Yemeni factions.

9. **Oman launches coral project.** Oman's Environment Authority began coral reef cultivation in the Daymaniyat Islands Nature Reserve, [Times of Oman reports](https://timesofoman.com//article/170469-environment-authority-launches-coral-reef-cultivation-project-in-daymaniyat-islands-nature-reserve). Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi met with Ukraine's National Security Council Secretary to discuss regional concerns. Oman reviewed bilateral cooperation agreements with Ukraine as the sultanate maintains its neutral diplomatic stance.

10. **Tourist spending hits Saudi record.** Saudi Arabia achieved record tourist spending levels, according to [Asharq Business](https://asharqbusiness.com/economics/127144/%D8%A5%D9%86%D9%81%D9%88%D8%BA%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%81-%D8%A5%D9%86%D9%81%D8%A7%D9%82-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%AD-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%8A%D8%B3%D8%AC%D9%84-%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%88%D9%89-%D9%82%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B3%D9%8A-%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF/). The milestone came during heightened regional tensions that could affect future tourism flows. Oman authorities separately seized 743 bottles of alcohol and tobacco products in Sohar, and arrested two people for drug trafficking in South Al Batinah governorate.
