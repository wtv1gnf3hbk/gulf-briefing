Good afternoon. Here's your Gulf briefing for June 2, 2026:

1. **US-Iran escalation.** The US [bombed Iranian](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-irael-war-kuwait-strikes-88daa9f90b48baaa7beb18e35515c59d) military sites after Iran fired missiles targeting American troops in Kuwait, according to AP. The US said it [struck Iranian](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/crlpy8n7pr6o?at_medium=RSS&amp;at_campaign=rss) radar sites as Iran targets American forces in Kuwait, the BBC reports. Sirens sounded over Kuwait City following the US attacks on Iranian positions.

2. **Bahrain travel ban.** Bahrain prohibited its citizens from traveling to Iran and Iraq due to ongoing security tensions, the Interior Ministry [announced](https://policemc.gov.bh/en/news/79019/). The move reflects regional concerns over escalating military activities. Sky News Arabia and Al Arabiya both reported the travel restrictions as urgent breaking news.

3. **Saudi condemnations.** The Saudi Foreign Ministry condemned Iranian attacks on Kuwait, calling them "sinful and repeated," according to the official Twitter account. Prince Khalid bin Salman offered condolences to former Yemeni President Abd Rabbuh Mansour Hadi's family following his death. Saudi Arabia also condemned Israeli aggression on Lebanese territory and Israeli incursions into Lebanon.

4. **Trump ceasefire claim.** Trump announced that Israel and Hezbollah have agreed to dial back fighting, according to [AP reporting](https://apnews.com/article/lebanon-israel-hezbollah-netanyahu-dahiyeh-rubio-ceasefire-airstrikes-a4708d5ed8d75f74463ba88c1cabca33). However, clashes continue in Lebanon despite both sides accepting a US partial ceasefire plan, the BBC reports. Israel's PM ordered strikes on Beirut suburbs as the Hezbollah conflict escalates.

5. **Yemen leadership changes.** Prince Khalid bin Salman announced plans to visit Sanaa with an Omani delegation to consolidate the truce and ceasefire, according to his Twitter account. Mohammed AlJabir, Saudi Arabia's envoy to Yemen, declared "Al-Hudaydah is liberated" and "Yemen is breathing." The IRG appointed Ahmed Hamid Lamlas as governor of Aden Governorate.

6. **OPEC+ output decision.** OPEC+ is likely to raise July oil output targets despite Hormuz disruption, [Reuters sources](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/opec-likely-raise-july-oil-output-target-despite-hormuz-disruption-sources-say-2026-06-01/) indicate. Oman oil prices [edged up](https://timesofoman.com//article/172483-oman-oil-price-edges-up-by-35-cents-to-touch-9176) 35 cents to $91.76. Asian imports of US crude have surged but cannot offset Hormuz losses, Reuters reports.

7. **Oman legal reforms.** Sultan Haitham issued two royal decrees: one promulgating a Law on Combating Cybercrime and another amending provisions of the Cultural Heritage Law, according to the Oman News Agency. Oman's foreign minister held discussions with Iran's deputy foreign minister, reaffirming diplomatic engagement on all fronts.

8. **UAE AI leadership.** The UAE ranks first globally in artificial intelligence talent concentration, growing 121% between 2019 and 2025, Sheikh Abdulla bin Zayed announced. Abu Dhabi Games partnered with Aspire to advance AI-driven game development. The UAE launched the Naseej National Initiative for Textile Circularity under President Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed's directives.

9. **Qatar government changes.** Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani was appointed Prime Minister by Emir Tamim, according to his Twitter account. Majed Al Ansari was named advisor to the PM and official spokesman for the Foreign Ministry. Qatar mourned the death of former minister Abdullah bin Hamad Al Attiyah.

10. **Regional Hajj success.** Prince Khalid bin Salman congratulated Saudi leadership on the exceptional success of this year's Hajj season, which embodied the Kingdom's position in serving Islam and Muslims. The Saudi Interior Ministry arrested 8,090 violators of residency, work, and border security regulations within a week. Three individuals were arrested for vehicle theft and arson in Oman's South Al Sharqiyah.
