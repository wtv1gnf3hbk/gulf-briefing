**Gulf Bureau Daily Brief - May 28, 2026**

1. **Iran-Kuwait missile attacks.** Iranian forces launched drone and missile attacks on Kuwait, which the Kuwaiti army said its air defenses intercepted, according to [Reuters reports](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/kuwaiti-army-says-air-defences-intercepting-hostile-missile-drone-attacks-2026-05-28/). Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, and Egypt condemned the attacks, with the Saudi foreign ministry calling them a violation of Kuwaiti sovereignty. The strikes occurred following US airstrikes on Iranian military sites.

2. **US-Iran escalation continues.** The United States carried out new strikes against Iranian military sites and drones, following earlier Iranian retaliatory attacks on American positions, [Reuters reports](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/us-carries-out-new-strikes-iran-against-military-site-official-says-2026-05-27/). Iran's Revolutionary Guard announced it targeted an American base in response to US strikes. The Iranian foreign ministry expressed solidarity with Oman after Trump threatened the country over Strait of Hormuz control.

3. **Former Yemeni president dies.** Abd Rabbuh Mansour Hadi, former president of the internationally recognized Yemeni government, died, prompting condolences from Saudi King Salman and Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman to the Yemeni government and people, according to [official statements](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-war-oil-may-28-2026-8f5ed2813ba63df7ae9ccbe991688d29). The IRG government mourned Hadi as a defender of the republican system against Houthi forces.

4. **Hormuz shipping disrupted.** Three oil and LNG tankers exited the Strait of Hormuz with their transponders turned off, [Reuters reports](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/three-oil-lng-tankers-exit-hormuz-with-transponders-off-2026-05-28/), indicating continued shipping concerns following escalating US-Iran tensions. Trump dismissed reports of a potential deal to secure the strategic waterway, stating the US was "not satisfied" with current arrangements.

5. **Saudi Hajj operations continue.** Hajj pilgrims performed rituals in soaring heat as Eid al-Adha celebrations began in Saudi Arabia, with Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman receiving well-wishers at the Royal Court in Mina Palace, [AP reports](https://apnews.com/article/islam-hajj-mina-saudi-arabia-muslim-908f0c90c0f2308496ab90997f78488a). The kingdom recorded 24.5 million calls in Mecca and holy sites on the tenth day of Dhul Hijjah.

6. **Hamas military leader killed.** Israeli airstrikes killed the new head of Hamas's military wing in Gaza City, Hamas confirmed, following previous strikes that targeted the group's leadership structure, [AP reports](https://apnews.com/article/israel-palestinians-gaza-hamas-war-eid-news-05-27-2026-4861f7c0c9cfda914007dfff975bae7a). The strike occurred as Israel continued operations against Hamas positions during Eid al-Adha.

7. **UAE tourism sector reviewed.** Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid reviewed achievements of the UAE's tourism sector for 2025 during government meetings in Abu Dhabi, according to the UAE Government Media Office. The review was part of broader government assessments following the UAE's artificial intelligence governance initiatives launched during the federal retreat.

8. **Iran restricts media access.** Iran expanded restrictions on distribution of news content by international media outlets, limiting foreign press operations as the country faced continued internet disruptions, [AP reports](https://apnews.com/article/iran-media-restrictions-reporting-distribution-ec8938bd08663f934fcf302695eeac99). Iranian officials said internet shutdown restrictions had ended following previous connectivity problems.

9. **Saudi-Yemen oil support.** Saudi Arabia approved $150 million in urgent oil derivatives support to the Yemeni government through the Saudi Development and Reconstruction Program, Yemen's information minister Mohammed Al-Eryani announced on social media. The assistance was provided under guidance from Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Defense Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman.

10. **Qatar diplomatic appointments.** Qatar's Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad appointed a new Prime Minister and foreign ministry spokesman, with Majed Al Ansari named as advisor and official spokesman for the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, according to [official announcements](https://timesofoman.com//article/172331-blatant-violation-of-international-law-tehran-condemns-alleged-us-ceasefire-breaches). Al Ansari denied reports that Qatar offered $12 billion to Iran for regional deals.
