**World in Brief: Gulf Region - April 4, 2026**

1. **Iran-UAE attacks.** Iran struck multiple Gulf targets Friday, with UAE air defenses [intercepting 18](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-03/abu-dhabi-halts-operations-at-main-gas-facility-after-attack) ballistic missiles, 4 cruise missiles and 47 drones. An Egyptian national died at Abu Dhabi's Habshan gas facility from falling debris after successful interceptions, forcing operations to halt. Minor damage occurred to buildings in Dubai Internet City and Marina areas from interceptor fragments.

2. **US aircraft downed.** Two US fighter jets were [shot down](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-israel-trump-lebanon-april-3-2026-a6365c6123cc8a696474f576d4ce7668) over Iran as the regional war escalated, according to AP News. At least one crew member remains missing while search operations continue. The downing marks a significant escalation in the Iran-US confrontation following weeks of mounting tensions.

3. **Saudi defense meetings.** Prince Khalid bin Salman conducted defense talks with ministers from Greece, Britain and Hungary this week, condemning Iran's "flagrant attacks" on the Kingdom and regional states. The Crown Prince received calls from Russian President Putin and Italian Prime Minister Meloni to discuss [regional escalation](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/italys-meloni-flies-gulf-region-energy-security-push-2026-04-03/) impacts on maritime security and the global economy.

4. **Bahrain internal unrest.** The Gulf kingdom cracked down on dissent as the Iran war [reignited protests](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-war-bahrain-protests-dissent-952f20a5bafd31d91b2a83454e8f9985), according to AP News. Bahrain's Interior Ministry arrested drivers for "causing disturbances" and "spreading panic" while air raid sirens sounded multiple times. Bahrain's Foreign Ministry postponed voting on a UN resolution regarding Strait of Hormuz navigation.

5. **Strait of Hormuz shipping.** Three vessels successfully transited the strategic waterway using a [new route](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-02/three-ships-appear-to-enter-hormuz-by-new-route-along-oman-coast) along Oman's coast, Bloomberg reported. A French-owned ship also passed through the strait despite ongoing regional tensions. The alternative routing aims to maintain commercial traffic while avoiding Iranian territorial waters.

6. **UAE banking liquidity.** The UAE injected $8 billion in liquidity to help domestic lenders cope with regional crisis impacts, [Jefferies reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-03/jefferies-says-uae-injected-8-billion-liquidity-to-help-lenders). The emergency funding aims to maintain financial stability as regional tensions threaten economic conditions across Gulf banking sectors.

7. **Yemen Houthi agreements.** Houthi spokesman Mohammed Abdulsalam announced an agreement to cancel recent banking decisions on both sides and resume Yemenia Airlines flights between Sanaa and Jordan. The deal increases flights to three daily and adds routes to Cairo and India, suggesting ongoing back-channel negotiations despite broader regional hostilities.

8. **Oman drowning incident.** A child died in a drowning incident in Al Khaboura, Times of Oman [reported](https://timesofoman.com//article/170228-child-dies-in-drowning-incident-in-al-khaboura). Separately, Oman's hockey team began Asian Games qualifiers with a 4-1 victory over Indonesia as sporting activities continued despite regional tensions.

9. **Zelenskyy Gulf visit.** Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy visited Gulf Arab states to discuss drone defense cooperation and seek strategic ties, [AP reported](https://apnews.com/article/russia-ukraine-war-odesa-drones-zelenskyy-gulf-5d520d03324170efbfb7f75ca6f2492e). The visit aimed to secure advanced defense technologies as Ukraine faces continued Russian attacks while Gulf states manage their own Iran crisis.

10. **Kazakhstan falcon restoration.** Kazakhstan released endangered saker falcons in a [Saudi-backed conservation](https://www.reuters.com/business/environment/kazakhstan-releases-endangered-saker-falcons-saudi-backed-restoration-drive-2026-04-03) drive, Reuters reported. The wildlife restoration program reflects Saudi Arabia's broader environmental initiatives under Vision 2030, continuing despite current regional security challenges.
