1. **Former Yemen president dies.** Abdrabbuh Mansour Hadi, former president of the Yemeni IRG who fled into Saudi exile in 2015 when Houthi forces seized Sanaa, died at age 80. Hadi led the internationally-recognized government during Yemen's civil war until the Presidential Leadership Council replaced him in 2022. His death prompted condolences from regional leaders, with Saudi Arabia [expressing sympathy](https://apnews.com/article/yemen-expresident-abdrabbuh-mansour-hadi-died-b3afd7b3679af14d335578e9560c7cd8) for the former ally.

2. **Kuwait air defenses intercept attacks.** Kuwait said its air defenses intercepted hostile missile and drone attacks, according to Reuters reports. The strikes followed escalating regional tensions between Iran and the US. Egypt condemned the attacks and affirmed [full solidarity](https://www.mfa.gov.eg/ar/Ministers/Details/MinisterActivities/5408) with Kuwait, while the Arab League chief strongly denounced Iran's aggression against the Gulf state.

3. **US-Iran deal emerges.** American and Iranian negotiators reached a tentative agreement to extend the ceasefire and begin fresh nuclear talks, according to AP reports. The deal follows recent military exchanges between the two countries. Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan said the kingdom "highly appreciates" President Trump's decision to give diplomacy another [chance to reach](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-war-oil-may-28-2026-8f5ed2813ba63df7ae9ccbe991688d29) an acceptable agreement.

4. **Three tankers exit Hormuz quietly.** Three oil and LNG tankers left the Strait of Hormuz with their transponders turned off, Reuters reported. The unusual move comes as regional tensions peaked following Iranian attacks on Kuwait and US military strikes. US Treasury Secretary warned Oman about potential tolls on the strategic waterway after Trump [threatened the sultanate](https://arabic.cnn.com/middle-east/article/2026/05/28/oman-has-assured-us-it-wont-toll-strait-of-hormuz-bessent-says) over Iran support allegations.

5. **Crown Prince hosts Hajj reception.** Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman held the annual reception for state dignitaries and pilgrimage delegations at Mina Palace as Hajj concluded. Interior Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Saud met with health officials and foreign ministers from Jordan and Malaysia during the pilgrimage season. The kingdom emphasized its role as [guardian of](https://twitter.com/spagov) the Two Holy Mosques.

6. **UAE artificial intelligence surges.** The UAE ranked first globally in artificial intelligence talent concentration, growing 121% between 2019 and 2025, Sheikh Abdullah bin Zayed announced. The Emirates also restored the historic Umayyad Mosque in Damascus with support from Sheikha Fatima bint Mubarak. The projects reflect the UAE's commitment to [building the future](https://twitter.com/ABZayed) and preserving Arab heritage.

7. **Qatar denies Iran payment reports.** Qatar's Foreign Ministry spokesman Majed Al Ansari said reports that Qatar offered $12 billion to Iran to secure a regional deal are "simply not true." The denial came as some parties attempt to sabotage diplomatic efforts toward regional de-escalation. Al Ansari was recently appointed as advisor to Qatar's Deputy Prime Minister and [Foreign Minister](https://twitter.com/majedalansari) spokesman.

8. **Bahrain condemns Kuwait attacks.** Bahrain strongly denounced the Iranian assault on Kuwait in a statement from the Foreign Ministry. The kingdom expressed full solidarity with Kuwait and called the strikes a flagrant violation of sovereignty. Bahrain's condemnation joined a chorus of Arab states [condemning Iran's](https://twitter.com/bahdiplomatic) aggressive actions against the Gulf neighbor.

9. **Oman celebrates Eid prayers.** Sultan Haitham bin Tarik performed Eid Al-Adha prayers at Sultan Qaboos Mosque in Sohar. Prince William received Omani Culture Minister Sayyid Theyazin bin Haitham at Windsor Castle to discuss UK-Oman relations. The meetings preceded the launch of the "Oman: The Jewel of Arabia" [expedition project](https://twitter.com/ONA_eng) in London.

10. **Air cargo rises despite disruptions.** Global air cargo demand increased 4% in April despite Middle East conflicts affecting regional routes, according to IATA. The growth demonstrates aviation sector resilience as geopolitical tensions continue affecting Gulf shipping lanes. Regional airlines maintained operations while adapting [to security](https://timesofoman.com/article/172334-air-cargo-demand-rises-4-in-april-despite-middle-east-disruptions-iata) challenges.
