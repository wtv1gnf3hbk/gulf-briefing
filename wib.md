**Gulf Regional Briefing**

1. **Iran nuclear facilities hit.** The International Atomic Energy Agency confirmed that Iran's Khondab heavy water reactor sustained severe damage and is no longer operational following Israeli strikes. The facility, which produced heavy water for nuclear research, was [rendered inoperable](https://timesofoman.com//article/170036-iaea-says-irans-khondab-heavy-water-plant-sustained-severe-damage-no-longer-operational-after-israeli-strikes) in what marks a significant blow to Iran's nuclear infrastructure. The IAEA announced its assessment after inspecting the damaged site.

2. **Saudi Arabia intercepts Iranian missiles.** Saudi air defenses successfully intercepted five ballistic missiles targeting the eastern region, according to the Ministry of Defense. Al Ekhbariya TV reported that calm prevailed in the area following the interceptions, with normal traffic movement resuming. 3. **UAE intercepts major missile barrage.** UAE air defenses engaged 16 ballistic missiles and 42 drones launched from Iran on March 29, according to the Defense Ministry. Six people were injured in Abu Dhabi after a missile was intercepted near the KEZAD economic zone. Since Iranian attacks began, UAE forces have [intercepted 414](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/five-injured-abu-dhabi-after-missile-intercepted-near-kezad-authorities-say-2026-03-28/) ballistic missiles and 15 cruise missiles targeting the emirates.

4. **Kuwait power plant attacked.** An Iranian attack on a power station in Kuwait killed one worker, according to CNN Arabic. The strike targeted critical infrastructure in the Gulf state as Iran expanded its regional military operations. Bahrain condemned the [sinful Iranian attacks](https://arabic.cnn.com/middle-east/article/2026/03/30/worker-killed-in-attack-on-kuwait-power-plant-news-agency-reports) that targeted Kuwait, calling for international action against Tehran's escalating aggression across the Gulf region.

5. **Pakistan hosts Iran peace talks.** Pakistan convened talks with foreign ministers from Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Egypt to discuss ending the Iran conflict. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif met separately with Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan to discuss regional stability. Bloomberg reported that the participating nations [support US-Iran](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-29/pakistan-says-saudi-egypt-turkey-support-us-iran-peace-talks) peace negotiations, with focus on proposals for managing the Strait of Hormuz crisis.

6. **Houthis launch Israel strike.** Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi movement fired missiles at Israel, with Israeli air defenses intercepting two projectiles according to Al Arabiya. The attack marked the Ansar Allah group's entry into the broader Iran conflict following weeks of Red Sea shipping disruptions. AP News reported the strike [stoked fears](https://apnews.com/article/iran-israel-us-houthis-yemen-dba2e2e2309f08547a3cbfdc2c367897) of renewed maritime attacks against commercial vessels transiting the strategic waterway.

7. **Gulf markets fall on conflict fears.** Most Gulf stock markets declined as investors worried about broader regional conflict spreading, Reuters reported. Crude oil and liquefied natural gas supplies face "worst-possible scenario" risks according to energy analysts. Bloomberg's Hormuz tracker showed Saudi crude [heading to Pakistan](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-29/hormuz-tracker-saudi-crude-heads-to-pakistan-in-rare-transit) in rare transit routes as shipping companies avoid the strategic strait.

8. **US loses surveillance aircraft.** The United States suffered its first combat loss of a valuable E-3 surveillance jet in a missile strike, Bloomberg reported. The destruction of the airborne early warning aircraft represents a significant military setback for Washington's regional operations. The loss occurred as [US troops arrived](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-29/strikes-continue-as-houthis-join-iran-war-and-us-troops-arrive) in the region to reinforce defensive positions against Iranian attacks.

9. **Dubai World Cup proceeds despite tensions.** The Dubai World Cup horse race proceeded with glamorous ceremonies but drew thinner crowds due to the Iran war, AP Photos showed. The event, celebrating its 30th anniversary, brought together the world's best horses and largest owners. Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid congratulated the United States on [Al-Khail Magnitude's victory](https://apnews.com/photo-gallery/dubai-horse-racing-photo-iran-war-0273e480caea3f71e0fd74b14031d935) in what he called the world's most important and expensive horse racing cup.

10. **Saudi Yemen envoy visits Sanaa.** Saudi Arabia's Yemen envoy Mohammed Al-Jabir announced he would visit Houthi-controlled Sanaa with an Omani delegation to consolidate the truce and discuss prisoner exchanges. On Twitter, Al-Jabir emphasized that nearly 2 million Yemenis work safely in Saudi Arabia, transferring over $4 billion annually to support 15 million family members. He described this as enhancing [Yemen's economy](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-israel-trump-lebanon-march-29-2026-26caaef651be1cb4d482b29adaa2d600) while benefiting the broader Yemeni population.
