Top News

Iran has effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz after 50 days of escalating tensions, bringing global oil flows to a virtual standstill and triggering emergency measures across the Gulf. Kuwait declared force majeure on oil shipments following Iran's abrupt reversal of a brief reopening, Bloomberg reports. The blockade has trapped nearly 600 million barrels of oil, with traffic slowing to a trickle as hopes for sustained access evaporated.

The crisis has prompted high-level diplomatic intervention, with Chinese President Xi Jinping calling for the Strait to remain open during a phone call with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, according to Bloomberg. Iran's senior leadership has doubled down on its position, with a politician telling the BBC that "Tehran will never cede control of Strait of Hormuz." Regional bourses have fallen on renewed US-Iran tensions, Reuters reports, while the USS Abraham Lincoln is enforcing a naval blockade near Iranian ports, according to the Times of Oman.

• Global strategic oil inventory totaled 2.5 billion barrels at the end of 2025, Reuters reports, highlighting the scale of reserves available as the Hormuz crisis intensifies.

• The White House indicated the US would likely help the UAE financially if needed, according to Bloomberg, as Gulf allies face mounting economic pressure from the shipping blockade.

• Middle Eastern bourses fell on renewed US-Iran tensions, Reuters reports, with UAE markets particularly affected by regional uncertainty.

UAE

Saudi Arabia
