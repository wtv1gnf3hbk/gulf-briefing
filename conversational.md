Kuwait experienced a significant escalation as Iranian forces launched [drone and missile](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/kuwaiti-army-says-air-defences-intercepting-hostile-missile-drone-attacks-2026-05-28/) attacks targeting the country, drawing widespread regional condemnation. The Kuwaiti army confirmed its air defense systems were intercepting the attacks, according to [Reuters reports](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/kuwaiti-army-says-air-defences-intercepting-hostile-missile-drone-attacks-2026-05-28/). Gulf states, the Arab League, and the Organization of Islamic Cooperation all condemned the attacks and expressed solidarity with Kuwait. The escalation comes as reports emerge of tentative diplomatic progress, with both [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/iran-us-trade-air-strikes-after-trump-dismisses-report-hormuz-deal-2026-05-28/) and [AP](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-war-oil-may-28-2026-8f5ed2813ba63df7ae9ccbe991688d29) reporting that Iran and the US have reached a preliminary agreement for new ceasefire talks and potential nuclear negotiations.

The attacks on Kuwait mark a significant expansion of the regional conflict, as the country had not been directly targeted in recent hostilities. Regional tensions are also affecting maritime traffic, with [three tankers](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/three-oil-lng-tankers-exit-hormuz-with-transponders-off-2026-05-28/) reportedly exiting the Strait of Hormuz with their transponders turned off, according to Reuters. The incident highlights the growing concerns about energy security in the region as the conflict spreads beyond its initial boundaries.
