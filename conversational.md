Iran's missile attack on Kuwait overnight sparked the most significant escalation in the Gulf in months, targeting US forces and prompting American retaliatory strikes against Iranian radar sites. [The BBC reports](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/crlpy8n7pr6o) that Iran fired missiles at American military positions in Kuwait as part of its broader campaign supporting Palestinian forces. The United States responded by [bombing Iranian](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-irael-war-kuwait-strikes-88daa9f90b48baaa7beb18e35515c59d) military installations, with sirens sounding across Kuwait City following the American strikes, according to AP.

The attacks represent a dramatic expansion of Iran's regional military operations beyond its traditional proxies. Saudi Arabia condemned both the Iranian strikes on Kuwait and Israeli operations in Lebanon in separate Foreign Ministry statements, with Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan [posting on Twitter](https://twitter.com/FaisalbinFarhan) that the Kingdom "highly appreciates" President Trump's decision to "give diplomacy a chance" to end the conflict and restore navigation security in the Strait of Hormuz. The Gulf states are now facing the prospect of direct Iranian military action against US allies in their immediate vicinity.
