Good morning from the Gulf. The United States has dramatically escalated tensions with Iran by announcing a naval blockade of all Iranian ports starting Monday, with President Trump specifically ordering the closure of the Strait of Hormuz following the collapse of peace talks in Islamabad. Oil prices have [surged past](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/4/13/oil-prices-surge-past-103-a-barrel-after-us-announces-blockade-of-iran) $103 per barrel, Al Jazeera reports, as markets brace for potential disruption to the world's most critical energy chokepoint.

The escalation comes as Saudi Arabia faces renewed security challenges. Riyadh has [summoned Iraq's](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/saudi-arabia-summons-iraq-envoy-over-drone-threats-iraqi-territory-2026-04-12/) ambassador over drone threats from Iraqi territory, Reuters reports, while the kingdom works to restore full capacity on its East-West pipeline following recent attacks. The Saudi Foreign Ministry confirmed on Twitter that it called in Ambassador Safiya Talib Al-Suhail "against the backdrop of the continued attacks and blatant threats that targeted the Kingdom and the sisterly Gulf states through marches launched from Iraqi territory."

**Energy & Economy**

• Oil markets are experiencing severe volatility as Brent crude [topped $103](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/4/13/oil-prices-surge-past-103-a-barrel-after-us-announces-blockade-of-iran) per barrel following the US blockade announcement, according to Al Jazeera, with natural gas prices also [surging higher](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-12/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-april-13) as Bloomberg reports the Hormuz crisis escalates.

• Saudi Arabia has [restored full capacity](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/saudi-arabia-restores-full-capacity-east-west-oil-pipeline-7-million-bpd-after-2026-04-12/) on its East-West oil pipeline to 7 million barrels per day after recent attacks, Reuters reports, with Princess Reema confirming on Twitter that "energy facilities and East–West pipeline affected by the attacks have recovered and restored operational capacity."

• Gulf stock markets are [sliding as](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/gulf-stocks-slide-us-iran-talks-falter-ceasefire-doubts-resurface-2026-04-12/) US-Iran talks falter and ceasefire concerns resurface, according to Reuters, with equities remaining subdued across the region.

• Oman has [launched bidding](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/oman-launches-bidding-round-five-oil-gas-blocks-2026-04-12/) for five new oil and gas exploration blocks, Reuters reports, as the sultanate seeks to boost energy production capacity.

**Country Watch**

**Saudi Arabia**
The kingdom is managing multiple diplomatic and security fronts as regional tensions escalate. Prince Khalid bin Salman held defense consultations with counterparts from Greece, Britain, and Hungary, with his Twitter posts emphasizing Iran's "continued flagrant attacks on the Kingdom and other regional states." The Saudi ambassador to Yemen, Mohammed AlJabir, announced plans to visit Sanaa with an Omani delegation "with the aim of consolidating the truce and ceasefire" and supporting prisoner exchanges.

**UAE**
Abu Dhabi is positioning itself strategically as the crisis unfolds. Sheikh Abdulla bin Zayed retweeted Sultan Al Jaber's warning about Red Sea shipping disruptions, noting "at least 22 ships were targeted" and "10 crew members were killed" since late February. The UAE continues economic diplomacy, with Crown Prince Khaled bin Mohamed visiting Beijing to strengthen the comprehensive strategic partnership with China, according to Abu Dhabi Media Office.

**Qatar**
Doha is emphasizing Gulf unity while managing regional tensions. Prime Minister Al Thani held separate meetings with UAE President Sheikh Mohammed bin Zayed and Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, stressing "the importance of intensifying joint coordination to protect the security of our countries," according to his Twitter posts. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Majed Al Ansari stated on Al Jazeera that "Iranian attacks have decreased but have not stopped" and Qatar rejects "any distinction between Gulf states."

**Bahrain**
The kingdom is taking precautionary measures following recent Iranian attacks. The Interior Ministry announced that "all public safety measures have been taken" and praised precautionary steps to maintain citizen safety. The Foreign Ministry condemned what it called a "terrorist plot that targeted the security of the State of Kuwait."

**Oman**
Sultan Haitham received a phone call from UK Prime Minister, according to Times of Oman, as the sultanate maintains its diplomatic mediation role. Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi emphasized on Twitter that "diplomacy and dialogue remain the only path to peace" following meetings with Ukrainian officials.

**Yemen**
Multiple factions are responding to the regional escalation. The internationally recognized government's Information Minister Mohammed Al-Eryani posted that "the Chairman of the Leadership Council stresses the importance of building on deterring the Iranian regime to end the threat of its militias in the region." Houthi spokesperson Mohammed Abdulsalam claimed Yemeni naval forces conducted reconnaissance operations while "an American warship opened fire in a hysterical manner." Southern Transitional Council leader Amr Bidh emphasized that "any political agreements will not stand in the way of the lives and livelihoods of citizens."

**Official Signals**

Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan co-chaired an international conference in Paris on Palestinian statehood with his French counterpart, posting that the kingdom's "position on the establishment of a Palestinian state is firm and unwavering." Meanwhile, Prince Khalid bin Salman's repeated emphasis on Iran's "flagrant attacks" in meetings with multiple defense ministers signals Riyadh's diplomatic campaign to build international support for its security concerns.

Qatar's Prime Minister Al Thani's Twitter posts about coordination with Saudi Arabia and UAE leadership suggest active Gulf diplomacy to manage the Iran crisis, while his spokesperson's Al Jazeera interview rejecting distinctions between Gulf states indicates Doha's position on collective security.

**Coverage Flags**

International outlets are providing more detailed coverage of the US-Iran blockade implications than regional Arabic media, which appears focused on domestic responses rather than global economic impacts.
